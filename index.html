      Musmapp

      Google Maps doesn't know you need to pray.

      A mobile-first app that helps Muslims in London find a mosque or prayer space quickly. It shows nearby mosques on a map, filters for female spaces, open now and wudu facilities, live prayer times and a Qibla compass.

      I built it to fix a gap in my own routine, then treated it as a full product exercise. The product work covers a PRD, user research, competitor analysis, a fake door test and a Figma prototype.

      The problem

      I grew up in Karachi, where mosques are on every corner but rarely have space for women. London has fewer mosques and more women's spaces, but no easy way to find them.

      There are over four million Muslims in the UK and no map built to help them navigate it. A student like Amirah, always moving between lectures and site visits, cannot tell from Google Maps which mosque has a women's space or whether it is open.

      The solution

      A map-first guide: find a mosque, filter for what you need, get there.

      • Map-first discovery. GPS-based search that widens from 3 km to 30 km and refreshes as you pan.
      • Smart filters. All, Female Spaces, Open Now and Wudu, plus search by name.
      • Live prayer times. A countdown to the next prayer, calculated for your location.
      • Mosque detail. Prayer grid, Jumu'ah times and facilities such as wheelchair access and parking.
      • Instant directions and a Qibla compass. One tap to Google Maps, and a compass that points to Makkah.

      How I built it

      I used Claude for problem framing, PRD review, synthetic customer development and prompt design, then built the app in Lovable. Prayer times come from the AlAdhan API, and mosque locations, map pins and directions from Google Maps.

      The prototype is honest about its limits. Female-space and wudu data currently comes from a small curated list, so I am building a database to hold facility details that Google Maps does not provide. The README in the repo lists the known limitations.
